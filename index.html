2 · Reading: Two Emerging Technologies (20 min)

Text A · The Internet of Things (IoT)

Imagine a school where lights turn off automatically when classrooms are empty. Sensors detect movement and send information to a central system. This is an example of the Internet of Things, or IoT. IoT connects physical objects to networks so they can collect and exchange data.

In a smart school, connected devices may help reduce electricity use. In a hospital, sensors could monitor the temperature of medicines. In a city, smart traffic lights might improve transport. However, these systems also create challenges. Connected devices need reliable networks, regular updates and strong security. If someone accesses a poorly protected sensor, private information may be at risk.

IT professionals will need to install, test and protect these systems. IoT offers opportunities, but good design must consider cost, privacy and sustainability.

Text B · Quantum Computing

Most computers use bits that represent 0 or 1. Quantum computers use quantum bits, called qubits. They work differently from ordinary computers and may help researchers solve certain complex problems. This does not mean that quantum computers will replace laptops or smartphones.

Scientists are exploring quantum computing for chemistry, materials research and other specialized tasks. In the future, quantum computers could help scientists model molecules more effectively. But today's systems are difficult to build and operate. Qubits are sensitive to noise, and errors are a major technical challenge.

Quantum computing may create new jobs in research, software and cybersecurity. IT professionals might need new skills to work with these technologies. It is exciting, but we should avoid the idea that every problem will become easy to solve.

3 · Language focus: Explain it to your teacher! (15 min)

Read the table. In pairs, prepare to explain the differences to your teacher. You may use Spanish to clarify the meaning, but give examples in English.

ModalUseExample
willPrediction presented as confidentIT professionals will need new skills.
mayFuture possibilityIoT may reduce energy use.
mightFuture possibility (often more tentative in context)Smart traffic lights might improve transport.
couldPossible future development or resultQuantum computers could help scientists.
Form: subject + will / may / might / could + base verb.
Example: “Sensors might save energy” (not “might saves”).
May, might and could overlap in meaning; they do not have fixed numerical probability levels.

Explain: Which modal sounds more confident? Which express possibility? What verb form follows each modal?

4 · Challenge: The Future Tech Consultant (25 min)

Worked example — from Text A

Client: A school wants to reduce electricity use.
Problem: Lights stay on in empty classrooms.
Solution: Install IoT movement sensors connected to smart lights.
Predictions: The system will detect empty rooms. It may reduce electricity use. Teachers might need training. The school could save money over time.
Risk: The system needs updates and secure network access.

Your turn: Work in pairs. Choose one client, propose a technology, explain one benefit and one risk, and write four predictions using all four modals.

Teacher note: the automatic review checks completeness and modal use, not whether the technical proposal is scientifically correct. Discuss the solution together.

Literature: The Machine Stops (1909)

Literary book trailer · The Machine Stops (1909)

Imagine a future where nobody needs to leave home. A powerful Machine provides food, music, information and communication. People live alone underground, but they can talk to anyone through screens.

Vashti trusts the Machine completely. Her son Kuno does not. He wants to see the real world and meet people face to face. Then strange technical problems begin. Can a society survive when everyone depends on one system?

One world. One Machine. What if it stops?

Original student-friendly teaser inspired by the story; not a quotation. It intentionally avoids revealing the ending.

Meet the author · E. M. Forster

Edward Morgan Forster (1879–1970) was an English novelist and short-story writer. He explored human relationships, social conventions and personal freedom. His novels include A Room with a View and A Passage to India. In 1909, he published The Machine Stops, a science-fiction story about technological dependence and human connection.

Think and explain: Forster wrote this story before modern computers and the Internet. Which technologies in his imaginary world resemble our world today?
